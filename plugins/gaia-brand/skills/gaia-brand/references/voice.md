# Voice

Gaia speaks to a tired, capable caregiver. Short, concrete, reassuring without being sentimental. It names the useful result and the next action. The user's judgment stays central; a baby's record is not a scorecard.

## Writing moves

Use human verbs: see, log, add, bring, share, choose. Prefer “feed” and “nap” to jargon. “Your baby” or a known child's name is better than “the user”; avoid assuming a mother/father pair when “caregivers” or “everyone” works. Interface sentences usually need one idea. Put steps in order and keep explanations adjacent to the action they clarify.

Headlines may carry a little personality, but paragraphs must explain the product. No generic “empowering your parenting journey,” competitive hype, guilt about missed logs, performance targets, or claims that Gaia knows a baby better than their caregivers.

| Instead of | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| Optimize your infant's sleep performance | See a likely window for the next nap. |
| Synchronize caregiver activity datasets | Everyone sees the same baby log. |
| Achieve the ideal growth percentile | Measurements in context. |
| Submit | Save feed / Create family / Choose a file |
| No data found | Nothing logged for this day yet. (Only for a true empty state.) |
| Something went wrong | We couldn't load your history. Try again. (Keep an exit/support path.) |
| Baby's name, repeated twice | First baby's name / Second baby's name |

## Sample brand lines

Existing shared-care campaign line: “Who fed her last? Everyone knows.” Adapt the pronoun/name for the intended story; do not hardcode it across the product.

Existing website hero: “Nobody has to ask when the last feed was.” Supporting copy names feeds, diapers, sleep, growth, and sharing. Preserve approved hero copy unless the task explicitly changes it.

Nap positioning: “Skip the nap math. Keep your judgment.” Explain a likely range based on this baby's recent sleep, not a magical exact minute or an age-based schedule. When the pattern is too thin or inconsistent, explain why there is no prediction without implying failure by the caregiver.

History: “The whole day, in order.” Trends: “Patterns without targets.” Growth: “Measurements in context.” Import: “Bring your history with you.” Do not fall back to obsolete “Bring your log across” or treat imports as exclusively Nara when other sources are supported.

## Truth boundaries

- Verify current pricing/platform/feature facts. “Free, no subscription” can describe the current product when confirmed; “no advertising business model ever” is not an evergreen promise.
- Do not claim everyone’s data is anonymous, “strict PII compliant,” GDPR-certified, or never shared with any service. Account and baby records are personal data; legal compliance and technical practices require current evidence and legal review.
- Future sponsorship/advertising/acquisition positioning must not promise absolute future outcomes. State the actual commitment accurately and separate contextual sponsorship from data-driven targeting. A brand skill cannot establish legal compliance or consent.
- Nap prediction is a planning hint, not medical advice. Do not say dietary logs diagnose iron deficiency, or growth percentiles show parenting success.
- Do not invent testimonials or portray generated actors as real customers. Only use quotes with a verified origin and appropriate permission.
- Comparisons can be persuasive and Gaia-first without fabricated competitor claims. Verify pricing/feature statements when publishing.

## Localization

Current source languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Brazilian Portuguese, Turkish. Verify coverage for the specific feature/release. Use endonyms in the selector: English, Español, Français, Deutsch, Italiano, Nederlands, Português, Türkçe. “Nederlands” is the language, “Netherlands” is the English country name.

Translate the intent and move headline breaks to suit the language. Allow longer labels to wrap; localize units, dates, pluralization, and 12/24-hour times. Keep product names and the custom wordmark unchanged. Website locale selection is explicit rather than forced by IP; app locale defaults follow supported device locale settings, with a user override. Do not infer someone’s language from their nationality or app-download country alone.
